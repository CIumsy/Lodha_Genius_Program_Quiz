Hours 1–2: Advanced Case Studies
----------------------------------

BCI Drone — Controlling a Tello Drone
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The **Tello Drone** connects to a laptop over Wi-Fi. A Python script listens for
classified biosignal events from the NPG Lite and sends flight commands to the drone.

**How it works:**

1. EMG or EEG signals are streamed wirelessly from NPG Lite to the laptop via BLE
2. A classification algorithm detects a specific gesture (e.g., jaw clench for EEG,
   or wrist flex for EMG)
3. The Python script maps that gesture to a drone command:

   - Flex left wrist → drone turns left
   - Flex right wrist → drone turns right
   - Double clench → drone hovers / lands

4. The command is sent over Wi-Fi to the Tello drone in real time

This is the same principle used in **research labs** where paralysed patients learn
to control robotic arms or computers using only their brain signals.

BCI Car — Wireless Muscle Control
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The **BLE BCI Car** is controlled by EMG signals from your forearm muscles, transmitted
wirelessly from NPG Lite:

- **Flex right forearm** → car turns right
- **Flex left forearm** → car turns left
- **Both forearms relaxed** → car stops

The car uses a simple threshold-based algorithm — no machine learning required. This
makes it fast and reliable enough for real-time control.

Hour 3: The "Mind Flayer" Experiment
--------------------------------------

This is the most dramatic demonstration in the workshop.

**The Human-to-Human Interface**

One person's muscle signals are used to involuntarily control another person's arm.

**How it works:**

1. **Person A** (the "controller") wears EMG electrodes on their forearm
2. Their EMG signal is processed and used to trigger a **muscle stimulator**
3. The stimulator sends a gentle electrical pulse to **Person B's** (the "receiver")
   arm muscles
4. Person B's arm moves — even if they try to resist — controlled by Person A's signals

**Important:** The stimulation is safe, non-painful, and brief. This experiment has been
demonstrated in neuroscience labs worldwide and was popularised by researchers at the
University of Washington.

**What this teaches:**

- The boundary between "my" body and "your" body can be bridged electrically
- The same principle underlies **functional electrical stimulation (FES)** used in
  medical rehabilitation to restore movement in paralysed limbs
- It raises fascinating questions about agency, identity, and the future of
  human-computer interfaces
