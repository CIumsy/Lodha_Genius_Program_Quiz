Day 2: Visualizing the Body (EMG & ECG)
========================================

**Objective:** Learn signal acquisition and how to reduce noise.

----

Hour 1: Recording Raw Bio-Data
--------------------------------

Before placing any electrode, you need to prepare your skin. Skipping this step is the
number one reason for noisy, unusable signals.

Why Skin Preparation Matters
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Your skin has a layer of dead cells and natural oils on the surface. These act like a
resistor (called **skin impedance**) between the electrode and the actual signal underneath.
High impedance = weak, noisy signal. Removing the outer layer fixes this.

**Steps to prepare skin:**

1. Identify the muscle or area you will record from.
2. Apply a small amount of **NuPrep gel** using a cotton swab.
3. Rub gently in circular motions to remove dead skin cells.
4. Wipe away the gel with an alcohol swab.
5. Let the skin dry for a few seconds, then place the electrode.

Understanding the Three Electrodes
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Every BioAmp recording uses three connections:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 15 85

   * - Electrode
     - Purpose
   * - **IN+**
     - Placed directly on the muscle/signal area. Captures the main signal.
   * - **IN−**
     - Placed 2–10 cm away from IN+, on the same area. Together with IN+, this cancels
       out electrical noise from the environment (like 50 Hz power-line interference).
   * - **REF**
     - Placed on a bony, electrically silent spot (elbow, wrist, or ankle). Acts as a
       reference ground to remove common interference.

The BioAmp uses the difference between IN+ and IN−. Because any external noise
(like 50 Hz from your phone charger) is present equally on both electrodes, it
cancels out — leaving only the real biological signal. This technique is called
**differential measurement**.

**Activity:** Electrode placement practice and visualising body signals using the
Chords Software Suite or Arduino IDE Serial Plotter.

Hour 2: EMG & Human-Computer Interaction
------------------------------------------

Electromyography (EMG)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

EMG records the electrical activity produced by your skeletal muscles. Every time a
muscle contracts — whether you clench your fist, raise your arm, or blink — motor
neurons send electrical pulses to the muscle fibres. EMG captures those pulses.

**Key numbers:**

- Signal range: 0–10 mV
- Useful frequency band: 50–150 Hz (within the 0–500 Hz full range)
- Amplified by ×1000 or more before reading

**Surface EMG (sEMG)** is non-invasive — you just stick gel electrodes on the skin
above the muscle. This is the type used in this course.

EMG and Human-Computer Interaction (HCI)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

EMG is one of the most powerful tools for HCI because muscles are easy to activate
deliberately and produce strong, reliable signals.

**Applications:**

- **Prosthetics:** Amputees control robotic limbs using residual muscle signals
- **Rehabilitation:** Therapists monitor muscle recovery after injury
- **Gaming:** Control games by flexing muscles instead of pressing buttons
- **Robotics:** Exoskeleton suits help paralysed people walk

**Algorithms used:**

- **Thresholding:** If the signal crosses a set voltage level, trigger an action
  (e.g., flex → click)
- **Envelope detection:** Smooth the raw EMG to detect overall muscle activation level

**Activity 1:** Visualise your EMG signals on LEDs using Muscle BioAmp Shield.

**Activity 2:** Listen to your muscle signals as audio through Muscle BioAmp Shield.

Hour 3: ECG & Heart Health
----------------------------

Electrocardiography (ECG)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Your heart is a muscle. Every heartbeat is triggered by an electrical impulse that
spreads across the heart, causing it to contract and pump blood. ECG records those
impulses from the surface of your skin — no surgery needed.

**Key numbers:**

- Signal range: 10 µV–5 mV
- Frequency range: 0.05–100 Hz

The ECG Waveform (PQRST)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

A single heartbeat produces a characteristic shape with five labelled points:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 20 80

   * - Feature
     - What it represents
   * - **P wave**
     - Upper chambers (atria) receiving the electrical signal and contracting
   * - **PR interval**
     - Signal travelling from atria down to the lower chambers (0.12–0.20 s)
   * - **QRS complex**
     - Lower chambers (ventricles) contracting — the main pump stroke (0.06–0.10 s)
   * - **ST segment**
     - Ventricles recovering after contraction (0.08–0.12 s)
   * - **T wave**
     - Ventricles resetting, ready for the next beat

The time between two R peaks (R-R interval) tells you your heart rate.

Real-World Applications
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

- Detecting irregular heart rhythms (arrhythmias)
- Diagnosing heart attacks
- Fitness trackers and smartwatches
- Portable ECG patches for continuous monitoring

**Compatible UDL hardware:** BioAmp EXG Pill, Heart BioAmp Candy, Neuro PlayGround Lite.

**Activity:** Visualise your heart signal on screen using BioAmp EXG Pill.
