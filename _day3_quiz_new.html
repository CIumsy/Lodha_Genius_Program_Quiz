Day 3 Quiz¶

Hour 1: EOG — Eyes as a Controller¶

Which brain wave is most prominent when you close your eyes and relax?
ADelta
BAlpha
CBeta
DGamma
Explanation: Alpha waves (8-12 Hz) are the signature of a calm, relaxed, awake state. They are strongest when your eyes are closed and disappear (alpha blocking) as soon as you open your eyes or start concentrating.
Your eye acts like a tiny battery. Which part carries the positive charge?
AThe cornea (front)
BThe retina (back)
CThe eyelid
DThe lens
Explanation: The cornea (front of the eye) is electrically positive, while the retina (back) is negative. This makes the eyeball a biological electrical dipole whose orientation changes as you move your eye.
To track whether a student is looking UP or DOWN, where should you place the electrodes?
ALeft temple and right temple
BAbove the eye and below the eye
COn the tip of the nose
DOn the back of the neck
Explanation: Vertical eye movements change the voltage difference along the vertical axis. Placing one electrode above the eye and one below captures this up-down dipole shift accurately.
Why does a blink show up as a massive spike on an EOG graph?
AThe brain shuts down completely.
BThe eyelid moving over the cornea creates a sharp electrical change.
CThe eye turns upside down.
DThe iris changes color.
Explanation: When the eyelid sweeps over the cornea during a blink, it rapidly changes the orientation of the eye dipole, creating a large and sudden voltage shift that appears as a sharp spike.
What is a major advantage of EOG over camera-based eye trackers?
AIt reads exact thoughts.
BIt requires less computer power and works even with eyes closed.
CIt does not need to touch the skin.
DIt shoots lasers to track movement.
Explanation: EOG requires only a few electrodes and minimal computing resources. It also continues to detect eye movement in complete darkness or even when the eyes are closed, unlike cameras.
While recording EOG, laughing causes messy, chaotic waves. What are these called?
ABrainwaves
BMuscle artifacts
CNeural networks
DVoltage drops
Explanation: Facial muscle contractions from laughing, talking, or chewing generate their own EMG signals that contaminate the EOG recording. These unwanted signals are called muscle artifacts.
Your EOG signal slowly drifts upward over time even when staring straight. How do you fix this in code?
AMultiply the data by a million
BTell the user not to blink
CUse a baseline correction or high-pass filter
DRestart the computer
Explanation: Slow baseline drift is common in biopotential recordings due to electrode-skin chemistry changing over time. A high-pass filter removes the slow drift while keeping the fast eye-movement signals intact. Baseline correction achieves the same effect.
Why do we need a ground electrode behind the ear or on the forehead?
ATo shock the user when they make a mistake
BTo cancel out ambient electrical noise from the room
CTo hold the headset in place
DTo measure skull temperature
Explanation: The ground (reference) electrode provides a common electrical baseline for the amplifier. Any ambient electrical noise present equally at all electrodes gets subtracted out, leaving only the true biological signal.
How does a threshold help an EOG algorithm?
AIt ignores tiny random eye tremors and only triggers on big, deliberate looks.
BIt randomizes the cursor movements.
CIt speeds up the hardware.
DIt saves battery life.
Explanation: Eyes naturally make tiny involuntary tremors called microsaccades. A threshold filters these out so only large, intentional looks trigger a command, preventing accidental activations.

Hour 2: Introduction to EEG & BCI¶

What are scalp EEG electrodes actually listening to?
AThe sound of blood rushing
BThe synchronized electrical activity of millions of neurons
CPhysical movements of skull bones
DEnglish words forming in the mind
Explanation: Individual neuron spikes are far too small to detect through the skull. EEG picks up the combined, synchronized electrical fields produced when large populations of neurons fire together, creating voltages detectable at the scalp.
What are the three essential steps in a Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) loop?
AType, click, print
BDrill, implant, shock
CRecord signals, decode meaning, output action
DRead, write, memorize
Explanation: Every BCI follows this pipeline: (1) acquire the raw brain signal with electrodes, (2) use algorithms to decode what the signal means, and (3) send the decoded command to a device such as a cursor, wheelchair, or robotic arm.
You are solving a difficult math problem under pressure. Which fast brainwave (12-30 Hz) increases?
ADelta
BTheta
CAlpha
DBeta
Explanation: Beta waves (13-30 Hz) are associated with active, analytical thinking, concentration, and alertness. Challenging cognitive tasks consistently increase beta power in EEG recordings.
How does a Neurofeedback meditation app help you calm down?
AIt gives you a mild shock when stressed.
BIt plays peaceful sounds only when your relaxation waves increase.
CIt forces your eyes open.
DIt flashes bright lights.
Explanation: Neurofeedback uses real-time EEG data to give the user instant feedback. When the app detects increasing alpha (relaxation) waves, it rewards the user with calming audio, reinforcing the relaxed brain state through conditioning.
Why must we use conductive gel or paste with EEG electrodes?
ATo glue the sensor permanently to the head
BTo bypass the electrical resistance of hair and dry skin
CTo cool down the brain
DTo dye the hair for tracking
Explanation: Hair and dry skin have high electrical resistance that attenuates the already tiny EEG signals (10-100 uV). Conductive gel fills the microscopic gaps between the electrode and skin, dramatically reducing impedance and improving signal quality.
Clenching your jaw during an EEG test ruins the data. Why?
AThe brain stops working.
BJaw muscles create massive electrical noise (EMG) that drowns out the brain waves.
CThe headset breaks under pressure.
DIt triggers deep meditation.
Explanation: Jaw and temple muscles produce EMG signals many times larger than brain waves. These muscle artifacts completely overwhelm the tiny EEG voltages, making the recording unusable until the participant relaxes their jaw.
If you want to capture brainwaves from the visual cortex, where should you place the sensor?
AOn the bridge of the nose
BAt the very back of the head (occipital lobe)
COn the tip of the ear
DOn the forehead
Explanation: The visual cortex is located in the occipital lobe at the very back of the head. EEG electrodes placed there (positions O1, O2, Oz in the 10-20 system) pick up visual processing signals most clearly.

Hour 3: HCI & BCI for Fun (EMG Games)¶

You play the Chrome Dino game by flexing your arm to jump. What signal is this?
AEEG
BEOG
CEMG
DECG
Explanation: Flexing your arm involves contracting skeletal muscles, which generates an EMG (Electromyogram) signal. The Muscle BioAmp Shield captures this signal and the thresholding algorithm triggers the jump command when the EMG crosses a set level.
Your Dino jumps constantly even when your arm is relaxed. What should you do to your code threshold?
ALower it to zero
BIncrease the threshold value so it requires a stronger flex
CDelete the code
DTurn off the monitor
Explanation: If the threshold is too low, even baseline noise or tiny involuntary muscle activity triggers a jump. Raising the threshold means only a deliberate, strong flex will cross it, eliminating false triggers.
What makes an HCI biosignal interface different from a standard game controller?
AIt does not use any electricity.
BIt reads body signals directly without needing a mechanical button press.
CIt only works when you are happy.
DIt cannot play video games.
Explanation: Traditional controllers require deliberate mechanical actions such as button presses or joystick movement. A biosignal HCI interface reads body electrical signals — muscle contractions, eye movements, or brain waves — directly, enabling hands-free or movement-free control.
Why is a muscle-controlled game (EMG) easier for beginners than a brain-controlled game (EEG)?
AMuscle signals are voluntary, strong, and easy to control instantly.
BThe brain does not make electricity.
CEMG signals do not use a computer.
DEEG waves are too large to capture.
Explanation: Muscles produce strong (millivolt-level), deliberate, instantly controllable signals. Brain waves are extremely small (microvolt-level), highly variable, and require training and practice to control reliably.
EOG, EEG, and EMG all measure body electricity. What is the ultimate biological source of these voltages?
AMetal wires in our veins
BStatic friction from walking on carpets
CThe movement of charged ions across cellular membranes
DTiny nuclear reactions in the stomach
Explanation: All biological electrical signals originate from the same fundamental process: ions (charged atoms like Na+, K+, Ca2+) flowing across the cell membrane through ion channels. This ionic current creates the voltage differences that electrodes detect on the skin surface.
