Day 2: Visualizing the Body (EMG & ECG)¶

Objective: Learn signal acquisition and how to reduce noise.

Hour 1: Recording Raw Bio-Data¶

Before placing any electrode, you need to prepare your skin. Skipping this step is the number one reason for noisy, unusable signals.

Why Skin Preparation Matters¶

Your skin has a layer of dead cells and natural oils on the surface. These act like a resistor (called skin impedance) between the electrode and the actual signal underneath. High impedance = weak, noisy signal. Removing the outer layer fixes this.

Steps to prepare skin:

  1. Identify the muscle or area you will record from.

  2. Apply a small amount of NuPrep gel using a cotton swab.

  3. Rub gently in circular motions to remove dead skin cells.

  4. Wipe away the gel with an alcohol swab.

  5. Let the skin dry for a few seconds, then place the electrode.

Understanding the Three Electrodes¶

Every BioAmp recording uses three connections:

Electrode

Purpose

IN+

Placed directly on the muscle/signal area. Captures the main signal.

IN−

Placed 2–10 cm away from IN+, on the same area. Together with IN+, this cancels out electrical noise from the environment (like 50 Hz power-line interference).

REF

Placed on a bony, electrically silent spot (elbow, wrist, or ankle). Acts as a reference ground to remove common interference.

The BioAmp uses the difference between IN+ and IN−. Because any external noise (like 50 Hz from your phone charger) is present equally on both electrodes, it cancels out — leaving only the real biological signal. This technique is called differential measurement.

Activity: Electrode placement practice and visualising body signals using the Chords Software Suite or Arduino IDE Serial Plotter.

Hour 2: EMG & Human-Computer Interaction¶

Electromyography (EMG)¶

EMG records the electrical activity produced by your skeletal muscles. Every time a muscle contracts — whether you clench your fist, raise your arm, or blink — motor neurons send electrical pulses to the muscle fibres. EMG captures those pulses.

Key numbers:

  • Signal range: 0–10 mV

  • Useful frequency band: 50–150 Hz (within the 0–500 Hz full range)

  • Amplified by ×1000 or more before reading

Surface EMG (sEMG) is non-invasive — you just stick gel electrodes on the skin above the muscle. This is the type used in this course.

EMG and Human-Computer Interaction (HCI)¶

EMG is one of the most powerful tools for HCI because muscles are easy to activate deliberately and produce strong, reliable signals.

Applications:

  • Prosthetics: Amputees control robotic limbs using residual muscle signals

  • Rehabilitation: Therapists monitor muscle recovery after injury

  • Gaming: Control games by flexing muscles instead of pressing buttons

  • Robotics: Exoskeleton suits help paralysed people walk

Algorithms used:

  • Thresholding: If the signal crosses a set voltage level, trigger an action (e.g., flex → click)

  • Envelope detection: Smooth the raw EMG to detect overall muscle activation level

Hour 3: ECG & Heart Health¶

Electrocardiography (ECG)¶

Your heart is a muscle. Every heartbeat is triggered by an electrical impulse that spreads across the heart, causing it to contract and pump blood. ECG records those impulses from the surface of your skin — no surgery needed.

Key numbers:

  • Signal range: 10 µV–5 mV

  • Frequency range: 0.05–100 Hz

The ECG Waveform (PQRST)¶

A single heartbeat produces a characteristic shape with five labelled points:

Feature

What it represents

P wave

Upper chambers (atria) receiving the electrical signal and contracting

PR interval

Signal travelling from atria down to the lower chambers (0.12–0.20 s)

QRS complex

Lower chambers (ventricles) contracting — the main pump stroke (0.06–0.10 s)

ST segment

Ventricles recovering after contraction (0.08–0.12 s)

T wave

Ventricles resetting, ready for the next beat

The time between two R peaks (R-R interval) tells you your heart rate.

Real-World Applications¶

  • Detecting irregular heart rhythms (arrhythmias)

  • Diagnosing heart attacks

  • Fitness trackers and smartwatches

  • Portable ECG patches for continuous monitoring

Compatible UDL hardware: BioAmp EXG Pill, Heart BioAmp Candy, Neuro PlayGround Lite.

Activity: Visualise your heart signal on screen using BioAmp EXG Pill.

Day 2 Quiz¶

Where should the REF electrode be placed?
AOn the belly of the muscle being recorded
BOn a bony area like the elbow or wrist
C2 cm away from IN+
DAnywhere on the skin surface
Explanation: The REF electrode must be placed on an electrically neutral site — a bony area with no muscle underneath, such as the elbow, wrist, or ankle. This gives the amplifier a stable reference point.
What does the QRS complex in an ECG represent?
AThe lower chambers (ventricles) contracting to pump blood
BThe upper chambers (atria) receiving a signal
CThe heart resetting between beats
DBlood flowing through the aorta
Explanation: The QRS complex represents ventricular depolarisation — the main pumping contraction of the heart lower chambers (ventricles). It is the largest feature on a normal ECG trace.
What technique does the BioAmp use to cancel out 50 Hz power-line noise?
AHigh-pass filtering only
BIncreasing the sampling rate
CPlacing the REF electrode further away
DDifferential measurement between IN+ and IN-
Explanation: Differential measurement amplifies only the difference between IN+ and IN-. External noise (like 50 Hz interference from mains electricity) is identical on both electrodes, so it subtracts to zero and disappears from the output.
Which situation would MOST likely create an EMG signal?
ABlinking eyes
BFlexing your bicep
CSleeping deeply
DListening to music
Explanation: EMG records electrical activity from skeletal muscles during contraction. Flexing your bicep is a direct muscle contraction, producing a clear EMG signal.
Which body signal would doctors MOST likely use to monitor heartbeat patterns?
AEMG
BEEG
CECG
DEOG
Explanation: ECG (Electrocardiogram) records the heart electrical activity, showing its rhythm and detecting abnormalities — making it the standard tool for monitoring heartbeat patterns.
Before recording body signals, why is skin preparation important?
ATo make the skin colorful
BTo reduce noise and improve signal quality
CTo charge the electrodes
DTo increase the signal frequency
Explanation: Proper skin preparation removes dead cells and oils that increase impedance, directly improving signal quality by reducing noise at the electrode-skin interface.
Electrodes act like:
ATiny cameras
BSignal collectors from the body
CBatteries
DSpeakers
Explanation: Electrodes are transducers — they sit on the skin and pick up the tiny electrical potentials produced by underlying muscles or the heart, converting them into signals the amplifier can process.
Which software can be used to visualize body signals?
AArduino IDE
BChords Software Suite
CBoth A and B
DMS Paint
Explanation: Both the Arduino IDE Serial Plotter and the Chords Software Suite can display real-time biosignal graphs from BioAmp hardware.
Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) means:
AHumans talking only to humans
BInteraction between humans and machines
CRepairing computers
DBuilding robots only
Explanation: HCI is the field of study and design focused on how people interact with computers and other digital devices — including using biosignals like EMG to control machines.
A gesture-controlled computer system would MOST likely depend on:
AEMG signals from forearm muscles
BEEG brain waves
CECG heart rhythms
DEOG eye movements
Explanation: Gestures involve muscle movement. EMG sensors on the forearm or hand can detect which gesture is being made and translate it into a computer command.

ARecord and visualise EMG signals from muscles
BRecord brain waves using EEG

DTrack eye movements using EOG
Explanation: The Muscle BioAmp Shield is designed to acquire, amplify, and visualise surface EMG signals, making it ideal for hands-on exploration of muscle activity.
LEDs connected to EMG signals can help us:
AHear colours
BVisually understand muscle activity
CIncrease internet speed
DCharge sensors
Explanation: By mapping EMG amplitude to LED brightness or number of LEDs lit, students get an immediate visual representation of how muscle contraction strength changes in real time.
Listening to muscle signals helps students understand that:
AMuscles produce electrical activity
BMuscles contain speakers
CMuscles store music
DSignals are invisible forever
Explanation: When muscle EMG is converted to audio, the crackling or buzzing sound during contraction makes it immediately clear that muscles generate electrical (and therefore audible) activity.

AHeart
BStomach

DSkin
Explanation: The heart beats continuously — roughly 60-100 times per minute — driven by its own electrical pacemaker (the SA node), regardless of whether you are awake or asleep.

Which signal is MOST useful for studying exercise intensity?
AECG
BEMG
CBoth ECG and EMG
DEOG
Explanation: During exercise, both EMG (muscle contraction effort) and ECG (heart rate) change significantly. Together they give a complete picture of exercise intensity from both muscular and cardiovascular perspectives.
Which of the following can reduce signal noise?
AProper skin preparation
BCorrect electrode placement
CStable connections
DAll of the above
Explanation: Good signal quality depends on all three: abrading the skin reduces impedance, correct electrode positioning captures the target signal, and secure connections prevent motion artifacts.
Why do biosignal graphs move up and down on screen?
ABecause the body produces changing electrical signals
BBecause the monitor is broken
CBecause gravity changes
DBecause Arduino draws randomly
Explanation: The graph traces the continuously changing voltage measured at the electrodes. As muscles contract or the heart beats, the electrical potential rises and falls, causing the waveform to move.
Which activity is BEST for observing ECG signals clearly?
ARunning fast
BSitting calmly
CJumping continuously
DShaking electrodes
Explanation: Sitting calmly minimises motion artifacts and muscle noise. Physical activity introduces large EMG signals and movement noise that can obscure the smaller ECG waveform.
