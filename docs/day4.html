Day 4: Gaming in the Field of HCI and BCI¶

Objective: Apply learned skills to play HCI & BCI games using your own body signals.

Meet the Neuro PlayGround Lite (NPG Lite)¶

The Neuro PlayGround Lite (NPG Lite) is a compact, all-in-one biosignal board designed for BCI applications. It is part of the Ninja Kit used in this course.

What NPG Lite can do:

  • Record EEG, EMG, ECG, and EOG signals

  • Send data wirelessly via BLE (Bluetooth Low Energy)

  • Connect to a laptop, phone, or Raspberry Pi without USB cables

  • Run simple classification algorithms to trigger actions in real time

Why BLE?

Wired connections limit movement and can introduce motion artefacts (noise from the cable moving). BLE lets you move freely while your signals are streamed to the computer, which is essential for games and robotics.

Applications of NPG Lite¶

Application

How it works

Click photo with eye signals

A deliberate upward eye movement (EOG spike) triggers the camera shutter

Play Dino game with eyes

A blink (detected as a sharp EOG pulse) sends a “jump” command to the game

Servo Claw game

Your grip strength (EMG amplitude) controls a servo motor claw — squeeze to close it, relax to open it. Stack objects to win!

Activity: The Servo Claw Challenge¶

The servo claw is connected to an Arduino that reads your EMG signal:

  1. Place the EMG electrodes on your forearm (IN+, IN−, REF as practised on Day 2)

  2. The Arduino reads the signal on analog pin A0

  3. A thresholding algorithm maps your grip force to the claw angle (0°–180°)

  4. Pick up small objects and stack them — the team with the tallest stack wins!

This is the same principle used in prosthetic hands for amputees.

Day 4 Quiz¶

What does BLE stand for?
ABrain Link Electronics
BBiosignal Level Encoder
CBluetooth Low Energy
DBiological Learning Engine
Explanation: BLE stands for Bluetooth Low Energy. It transmits data wirelessly over short distances while using very little battery power — ideal for wearable biosignal devices.
In the Servo Claw game, which biosignal controls the claw?
AEMG (muscle signal from the forearm)
BEEG (brain waves)
CECG (heart signal)
DEOG (eye movement)
Explanation: EMG (muscle signal) from the forearm is used. The amplitude of the EMG signal corresponds to how hard you are squeezing — stronger grip = higher EMG amplitude = claw closes further.
Why is wireless (BLE) transmission preferred over a wired USB cable for BCI games?
ABLE signals have higher resolution than USB
BIt allows free movement and avoids motion artefacts from cable pulling
CUSB cannot transmit biosignals at all
DBLE automatically filters noise before transmission
Explanation: A cable physically restricts movement and can pull on the electrodes, generating motion artefacts (false signals from the cable moving). Wireless BLE removes both problems, allowing free movement and cleaner signals.

Explanation: The NPG Lite has an onboard Bluetooth / Wi-Fi module, allowing it to stream biosignals wirelessly to a laptop, phone, or web browser without any USB cable — essential for free-movement BCI games.
What cross-platform software developed by Upside Down Labs allows you to view your signals directly in a web browser without installing anything?
ASpike Recorder Mobile
BChords Software Suite (Chords-Web)
CArduino Legacy Serial Monitor
DPython Signal Studio
Explanation: Chords Software Suite (Chords-Web) is a browser-based tool built by Upside Down Labs. It connects directly to the NPG Lite over BLE or serial and visualises your biosignals in real time — no installation required.
To update the firmware binaries over a simple command-line interface on your NPG Lite board, which utility do you use?
ANPG Lite Flasher
BBioAmp Solder Bridge
CChords LSL Visualizer
DWindows Device Manager
Explanation: NPG Lite Flasher is the dedicated command-line tool for flashing updated firmware onto the NPG Lite board. It simplifies the update process to a single command without needing the Arduino IDE.
What built-in feature protects users from sudden electrostatic shocks when handling electrodes on the NPG Lite?
AOnboard rubber coating
BUser ESD Protection components
CA 5-meter physical distance from AC appliances
DAuto-turnoff power dial
Explanation: The NPG Lite includes dedicated ESD (Electrostatic Discharge) Protection components on its input lines. These clamp any high-voltage transients — such as static from touching electrodes — before they can damage the sensitive analog front-end circuitry.

Section 2: Bio-Potential Signal Basics & Kit Safety¶

By default, the BioAmp EXG Pill inside the DIY Neuroscience Kit Pro is pre-configured to record which two signals?
AEMG (Muscles) and ECG (Heart)
BEEG (Brain) and EOG (Eyes)
CVoice and Acoustic commands
DTemperature and Pulse rate
Explanation: Out of the box, the BioAmp EXG Pill is configured for EEG (Brain) and EOG (Eyes) recording. Its high-gain, low-noise front-end is optimised for these very small signals in the microvolt range.
If you want to modify your BioAmp EXG Pill to specifically record clean muscle (EMG) or heart (ECG) data, what hardware step is recommended?
ABridge the specific configuration pads with a solder joint
BIncrease the input battery voltage to 24V
CConnect the reference cable to your nose
DRemove the instrumentation amplifier entirely
Explanation: The BioAmp EXG Pill has configuration solder pads on the PCB. Bridging the correct pads changes the gain and filter settings, reconfiguring the pill for the higher-amplitude EMG or ECG signals.
For the absolute best biopotential signal acquisition with any BioAmp setup, what rule must you follow regarding your laptop?
AConnect it to a high-speed Ethernet wall wire.
BEnsure your laptop is unplugged from the charger and runs purely on battery power.
CKeep it stacked directly on top of an AC appliance.
DTurn the monitor brightness up to 100%.
Explanation: Laptop chargers inject 50Hz or 60Hz AC noise into the chassis and ground plane. Running purely on battery power eliminates this noise source, dramatically improving the signal-to-noise ratio for small EEG and EOG signals.
How far away should you sit from any working AC household appliances to avoid 50Hz/60Hz background grid noise?
AAt least 50 centimeters
BAt least 5 meters
CExactly 100 meters
DDistance does not matter
Explanation: AC appliances radiate electromagnetic fields that couple into electrode cables and skin, adding 50Hz (or 60Hz in some countries) interference. Sitting at least 5 metres away keeps this interference below the noise floor of the amplifier.
Why do we rub a small drop of NuPrep Skin Preparation Gel onto the target skin spot before sticking electrodes?
ATo insulate the skin from any electricity.
BTo safely remove dead skin cells and dirt, lowering electrical resistance.
CTo act as a sweet-smelling permanent glue.
DTo test if the user is allergic to the plastic.
Explanation: The top layer of skin (stratum corneum) is made of dead, keratinised cells that create high electrical resistance. NuPrep gently abrades these cells away and removes surface oils, reducing skin impedance so the electrode picks up a clean, strong biological signal.

Section 3: Eye Controls (Click Photo & Dino Games)¶

When playing the Dino game or snapping a photo hands-free using your eyes (EOG), what physical phenomenon causes the massive voltage spike on your screen?
AHeart rate acceleration
BMuscle twitches in your fingers
CThe eyeball rolling slightly upward during a blink (Bell's Phenomenon)
DSudden high-level concentration changes in the brain
Explanation: During a blink, the eyeball rolls slightly upward — a reflex known as Bell's Phenomenon. The cornea (positive pole) moves rapidly away from the lower electrode, creating a large, sudden change in the cornea-retina potential that appears as a sharp spike on the EOG graph.
To record VERTICAL eye movements (looking up and down or blinking), how should your IN- and IN+ electrodes be placed?
ALeft temple and right temple side-by-side
BDirectly above and below a single eye
CBoth on the bony part behind your earlobe
DFlat on the forehead space
Explanation: Vertical eye movement shifts the cornea-retina dipole along the vertical axis. Placing one electrode directly above the eye and one directly below captures the full magnitude of this vertical voltage change.
If you are setting up a HORIZONTAL eye-tracking interface to move a game cursor left and right, where do the active sensors go?
AAbove and below the eye
BOn the right side and left side of the eye temples
COne on each elbow bone
DDirectly on the bridge of the nose
Explanation: Horizontal eye movement shifts the cornea-retina dipole along the horizontal axis. Placing electrodes at the outer corners of each eye (left temple and right temple) captures the left-right voltage polarity change precisely.
When using the EOG setup, where should the Reference (REF) electrode cable always be connected?
AOn a fleshy part of your bicep
BOn a bony part of your body, like the back side of your earlobe (mastoid)
CDirectly on the laptop's metal casing
DLeft hanging loose in the air
Explanation: The REF electrode must be placed on an electrically neutral, bony site — such as the mastoid (the bony bump behind the ear) — where there is no muscle activity. This gives the differential amplifier a stable ground reference, cancelling common-mode noise.

Section 4: Flexing Controls (Muscle BioAmp Shield & Servo Claw)¶

Unlike standalone sensors, what makes the Muscle BioAmp Shield incredibly user-friendly for an Arduino Uno user?
AIt connects via a 10-meter auxiliary cord.
BIt is an all-in-one shield that stacks cleanly directly on top of the Arduino Uno.
CIt reads thoughts instead of muscle contractions.
DIt does not require any wires or electrodes.
Explanation: The Muscle BioAmp Shield is designed as an Arduino shield — it plugs directly onto the Arduino Uno's header pins, forming a single stacked unit with no extra wiring required between the amplifier and the microcontroller.
What onboard visual indicator does the Muscle BioAmp Shield feature to show your real-time muscle strength without looking at a laptop monitor?
AA tiny LCD text screen
BAn onboard LED Bar Graph that lights up higher the harder you flex
CA laser projector pointing at the wall
DA single flashing yellow light
Explanation: The Muscle BioAmp Shield has an onboard LED Bar Graph — a row of LEDs that progressively illuminate as EMG amplitude increases. The harder you flex, the more LEDs light up, giving instant visual biofeedback without any software.
To listen to the raw crackle of your muscle fibers flexing in real-time, what can you plug into the 3.5mm onboard audio jack of the BioAmp hardware?
AA wireless Wi-Fi mouse
BA speaker or a pair of wired earphones
CA smartphone charger cord
DA 9V power block adapter
Explanation: The BioAmp hardware routes the amplified EMG signal to a standard 3.5mm audio jack. Plugging in wired earphones or a small speaker lets you hear the characteristic crackling sound of motor unit action potentials firing as you flex.
When connecting the 3D-printed Servo Claw to the Muscle BioAmp Shield to play the stacking game, what type of pins are you plugging into?
AHigh-voltage AC mains outlets
BOnboard hobby servo output headers
CUSB-C digital input ports
DAudio auxiliary ports
Explanation: The Muscle BioAmp Shield exposes standard 3-pin hobby servo headers (Signal, VCC, GND). These are the universal connector type used by all hobby RC servos, making it straightforward to connect the servo claw without any special adapters.
If your 3D-printed claw won't close all the way when you apply maximum forearm grip strength, how can you tune it?
ASqueeze until your arm hurts.
BModify the servo target angle mappings inside the Arduino IDE sketch file.
CAdd more gel stickers over your ears.
DWrap the claw in insulating duct tape.
Explanation: The Arduino sketch maps EMG amplitude values to servo angles using a map() function or a custom range. Adjusting the target angle values in the code — for example, changing the maximum angle from 90 to 150 degrees — allows full closure without any hardware changes.
When using the alternative dry-electrode Muscle BioAmp Band instead of sticky gel patches, where should the IN+ and IN- metal snaps face?
APressed against the bony tip of your elbow
BSnapped tight over the arm near the ulnar nerve line
CWrapped snugly around your forehead
DTied around the palm of your hand
Explanation: The Muscle BioAmp Band is designed to be wrapped around the forearm with the metal snap electrodes pressed flat against the skin over the muscle belly. The correct placement is along the ulnar nerve line on the inner forearm for reliable EMG capture.
